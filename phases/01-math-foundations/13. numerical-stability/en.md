Floating point is a leaky abstraction. exp() overflows above ~88 in float32, underflows below ~-104, and subtraction of nearly equal numbers amplifies rounding error catastrophically. The universal fix is the max-subtraction trick: subtract max(x) before exponentiating, then add it back outside the log. This makes softmax, log-sum-exp, and cross-entropy safe for any input magnitude. Always use stable implementations, never compare floats with ==, verify analytical gradients with centered finite differences, clip gradients by norm, use bfloat16 (not float16) for training, and check for NaN/Inf after every forward pass during debugging.
